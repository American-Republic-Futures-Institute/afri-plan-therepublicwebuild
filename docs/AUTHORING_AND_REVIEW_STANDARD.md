# The Republic We Build — Authoring and Review Standard

**Document:** ARFI-PLAN-STD-001  
**Version:** 0.1.0 (proposed)  
**Status:** Editorial standard under review; not institutional ratification  
**Applies to:** Future-state domain chapters, institutional/office profiles, conceptual pathway chapters, and associated public research notes

## 1. Purpose and editorial boundaries

*The Republic We Build* is an accessible, public-facing, long-term inquiry into the national conditions Americans might seek and the capabilities and responsibilities that could help sustain them. Its purpose is to establish **what future outcomes should be possible** and explain how they connect, not to prescribe particular government implementations before there is evidence, lawful authority and public deliberation.

**Lead with the future, work backward to the necessary conditions, and introduce current-state evidence only to explain a meaningful gap or uncertainty.** A manuscript chapter is not an agency audit, a legislative proposal, an appropriations request, a schedule, or an endorsement of a particular administration or party.

The initial chapter structure follows the proposed [Master Manuscript Outline](../MANUSCRIPT_OUTLINE.md) v0.2.0 in draft PR #3. This standard may be reviewed independently; the outline itself remains subject to change.

### Separation of document types

| Editorial artifact | Owns | Must not become |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundational principles | Enduring commitments, rights distinctions, constitutional guardrails | A pretext for unconditional powers or already-ratified policy |
| **Future-state domain chapter** | Human outcomes, conditions for progress, enabling capabilities, tradeoffs and open research | An office-by-office implementation program |
| **Institutional / office profile** | Lawful responsibilities, future contribution to domains, accountable interfaces and open options | A complete administrative audit or assumed agency reorganization |
| Conceptual pathway chapter | Capability dependencies, plausible alternatives, risks, changing conditions | A time-bound delivery plan |
| Research record | Sources, methods, data, legal interpretation, economic assumptions and dissent | Public-facing narrative prose copied into every chapter |
| Implementation dossier (later) | Specific lawful action, staffing, finance, sequence, tests and contingencies | An implicit commitment produced by publication of the main manuscript |

## 2. People-centered vocabulary

Use understandable language in the primary manuscript. Terms such as *capability*, *outcome*, and *institutional relationship* should be defined once in context:

- **Desired future condition:** What citizens and communities should be able to experience or accomplish.
- **Outcome:** An observable improvement or enduring condition that matters to people; not merely a count of government activity.
- **Enabling capability:** Something a society, market, organization or public institution must be able to do for an outcome to be feasible.
- **Condition for progress:** An outcome or capability that must exist and be sustained; **not** an arbitrary calendar milestone, mandatory program, or claim that a certain approach is the only solution.
- **Conceptual pathway:** One or more plausible combinations of institutions, market responses, resources and lawful decisions that could enable progress.
- **Government role:** What a competent legal authority could contribute. Do not infer legal power from a promising outcome.
- **Supporting evidence:** Dated, geographically relevant and inspectable sources, including opposing explanations and known limitations.

Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework (FEAF) models may support structured research; the main paper should explain their purpose in ordinary language and link technical details rather than requiring readers to understand the framework.

## 3. Required order of a future-state domain chapter

1. **The future we seek:** A concrete, inviting description of the desired national condition and its relevance to people.
2. **What success would mean:** Observable results, relevant populations and serious countermeasures against misleading averages.
3. **What must become possible:** Required capabilities without prematurely assigning delivery to a particular provider or agency.
4. **What we know and still need to learn:** Concise verified current context; evidence limits; hypotheses.
5. **Possible pathways and dependencies:** Non-exclusive ways to advance, including improving existing systems; no mandated implementation.
6. **Conditions for progress:** Outcome-based checkpoints, prerequisites and indicators; no unverified numeric targets or deadline commitments.
7. **Rights, costs and unintended consequences:** Existing protections, proposed rights, affected populations, fiscal/operational tradeoffs and institutional-abuse risks.
8. **Institutional relationships:** Links to legislative, executive, judicial and intergovernmental future-role profiles.
9. **Unresolved questions and public review:** What remains debatable, what evidence could change the analysis, and how contributions will be considered.

A chapter may reorganize subheadings for clarity, but these nine questions must remain answerable in its text or linked supporting record.

**Proportionality guideline:** The envisioned future, what success entails and the necessary capabilities should ordinarily form the largest portion of a chapter. Current-state description should remain concise and focused. This is an editorial principle, **not** a mechanical word-count quota.

## 4. Institutional and office profile standard

Each institutional profile must distinguish:

- **Existing structure and authority** (verified at a specified date and level of jurisdiction).
- **Future contributions** that *could* support one or more domain outcomes.
- **Required capabilities and relationships**, without presuming organizational continuity or prescribing a restructuring.
- **Constitutional and statutory bounds** on any proposed change in roles.
- **Accountability and protection** for the public, including appeal, oversight and limits on coercive power.
- **Options and open decisions** where an office or responsibility may evolve.

The branch index is a reader-navigation device, not a claim that every independent entity fits cleanly beneath a Cabinet department. Tribal governments, states, territories and localities must be represented according to their distinct legal standing.

## 5. Evidence and uncertainty protocol

Tag material assertions in research records as one of the following:

| Class | Editorial meaning |
| --- | --- |
| FACT | Empirically supported observation; source, date, population and scope required |
| LAW | Existing legal authority or interpretation; jurisdiction, controlling text and material uncertainty required |
| NORMATIVE | Proposed value, right or obligation; not stated as an empirical discovery or existing legal guarantee |
| HYPOTHESIS | Proposed causal relationship; falsifiable assumptions and verification plan required |
| SCENARIO | Conditional description of what may happen; not a prediction |
| CONTESTED | Disputed factual or interpretive claim with unresolved evidence |
| OPTION | Plausible future pathway, not an adopted action |
| RECOMMENDATION | An expressly reviewed proposal with legal, evidentiary and public-review provenance |

Source integrity rules:

- Prefer primary law, official records, reproducible statistics and methodologically credible studies, while considering firsthand public experience as meaningful evidence of needs and effects.
- Cite evidence near the claim. Explain whether any conclusion is a direct finding, inference or value judgment.
- Record publication date and relevant period for time-sensitive research; do not treat older baselines as current.
- A simulation describes outcomes under assumptions; it cannot independently certify those assumptions or prove a future prediction.
- Material disagreements and uncertainty must be preserved. No political affiliation, donor, source prestige or AI output independently settles an argument.
- Distinguish existing constitutional protections from proposed social guarantees and from aspirational national objectives.

## 6. Rights, institutional authority and policy neutrality

The paper can articulate stable normative commitments without binding itself to a single political party or preferred instrument. It should examine strengths in existing public institutions alongside genuine opportunities to evolve; never portray a department or workforce as failing merely because a new future-state concept is proposed.

- **Authority:** Explain which body can lawfully act before describing how it might act; if uncertain, label research pending.
- **Rights:** A desirable aggregate benefit cannot itself authorize violating a binding protection.
- **Federalism:** State, federal, tribal, territorial and local responsibilities require separate analysis rather than assumed federal direction.
- **Disagreement:** Treat informed public opposition as relevant evidence and democratic participation. Responses should listen, explain and, when justified, revise the analysis.
- **Options:** Include maintenance or improvement of existing approaches where credible; do not design an artificial choice between two ideological extremes.
- **Safety:** Examine both the threat addressed by a capability and the possibility of harm or abuse from the capability itself.

## 7. Heading, identity and traceability conventions

Use one H1 title per Markdown file. Major chapter sections are H2; subordinate questions are H3. H4 is optional and must never skip a heading level. Avoid headings that are only internal notation: combine a stable ID with reader-friendly language.

- Foundational principle ID: `FP-01` … `FP-20` (as presently proposed).
- Domain identifier: `DOM-01` … `DOM-14` (navigation taxonomy proposed in outline v0.2.0).
- Legacy source chapter ID: retain unchanged; see the 127-entry outline crosswalk in PR #3.
- Institutional profile ID: reserve stable references after the branch/office registry has been reviewed; **do not assign invented statutory authority to a new identifier**.
- Outcome measure and capability IDs: eventually held in the separate architecture repository. The manuscript links to the canonical entry; it does not duplicate the authoritative model.

At minimum a substantial chapter must link: relevant principle → desired outcome → required capability → potentially responsible institution(s) → evidence and uncertainty → public-review record. Detailed implementation sequencing is **not** mandatory for conceptual publication.

Use **The Republic We Build** consistently as the initiative/manuscript title. A historical source identifier may retain its original immutable characters for archival traceability without being used as a current public title.

## 8. Public participation and editorial disposition

Reader commentary should be associated with a stable section/paragraph and the exact version being criticized. Serious issues should receive a status such as *accepted*, *partially accepted*, *requires research*, or *not adopted with reasons*, subject to editorial capacity. Every comment need not receive a bespoke response, but consequential positions should remain visible and reviewable.

Moderation should address harm, impersonation, spam and manipulation rather than suppressing reasonable disagreement. AI may draft summaries of comments but must not automatically ratify editorial conclusions.

## 9. Definition of done for an editorial increment

An increment is ready for a **draft review PR** when all of the following hold:

- [ ] The desired future condition and human relevance are explicit.
- [ ] The distinction among fact, inference, norm, hypothesis, option and recommendation is preserved.
- [ ] Progress is characterized as outcomes or capabilities, not arbitrary dates or predetermined programs.
- [ ] Relevant existing rights, lawful authorities and jurisdictional boundaries are not misstated.
- [ ] Potential harms and unequal burdens are acknowledged rather than obscured by national averages.
- [ ] Competing explanations, feasible alternatives and uncertainty are visible.
- [ ] Domain-to-institution links are present where they are grounded; unverified responsibilities are identified for research.
- [ ] Public-facing text is understandable without specialist architecture knowledge and avoids unnecessary partisan or institutional accusation.
- [ ] References, stable identifiers, document status, internal links and Markdown heading hierarchy have been checked.
- [ ] Public-review questions and a correction path remain available.

**Publication/rating distinction:** Passing this checklist is a draft editorial acceptance gate. It does not ratify policies, legally approve institutional changes, establish empirical proof of outcomes, or make a research manuscript binding on government.
