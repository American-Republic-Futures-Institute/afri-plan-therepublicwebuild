## 1. Why this register exists

This record supports the companion [education institutional crosswalk](../manuscript/institutions/education/00-institutional-crosswalk.md) and the [education future-state chapter](../manuscript/02-education-and-lifelong-learning.md). The public manuscript describes desired educational outcomes and conceptual institutional relationships; this file records the sources and legal boundaries needed to avoid assigning powers to institutions that do not hold them.

The relevant institutions do not form a unitary education command. Congress legislates, funds and oversees within constitutional limits. Executive authorities administer specific laws and funds. State and local authorities hold primary K–12 governance responsibilities. Tribal governments have their own sovereign relationships, and territorial arrangements vary. Courts adjudicate cases within jurisdiction; they are not a national education planning agency. Families, educators, providers and students are contributors and rights-holders, not merely public-program components.

**Current structure is a dated research reference, not a guarantee of unchanged office names or delegations.** In 2026 the Department of Education publicly described interagency partnerships for education program administration while indicating that statutory eligibility, authorized funding and applicable protections remain in effect. The current operational point of contact for a particular program may therefore differ from its statutory accountable authority; later research must confirm program-by-program agreements and their legal scope. ## 2. Authority interpretation rules

| Distinction | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Constitutional/statutory authority | Identify enacted law and competent decision maker before describing a present governmental power. |
| Program administration | Identify the administrator and, separately, the source of its legal authority; an interagency agreement is not assumed to amend statute. |
| Congressional committees | Committees have legislative and oversight jurisdiction under chamber rules, not unilateral authority to enact laws or administer schools. |
| State/local government | Do not treat a federal grant, data request or desired outcome as general federal control of school curriculum or personnel. |
| Judiciary | Federal courts address properly presented cases and controversies; judicial remedies depend on jurisdiction and controlling law. |
| Tribal sovereignty | Distinguish tribally controlled schools from BIE-operated schools and avoid treating tribal nations as state subdivisions. |
| Territories | Verify each territory and statute; eligibility and governmental structure cannot be inferred from a generic state model. |
| Existing rights | Federal protections arise under their own conditions; education is not universally an expressly guaranteed fundamental federal constitutional right. |
| Proposed future roles | May express possibilities but do not become effective authority, financial obligations or policy merely by publication. |

## 3. Source registry

### EIA-SRC-01 — Federal/state/local education relationship

**Primary agency explanation:** [U.S. Department of Education — Federal Role in Education](https://www.ed.gov/about/ed-overview/federal-role-in-education?lang=en).

**What it supports:** Education is primarily a state and local responsibility; federal involvement supports distinct functions. State and local bodies, public and private institutions, and communities establish schools, curricula, and enrollment/graduation requirements.

**Limit:** The page cites historical school-year 2012–13 finance figures; those cannot be presented as the current financing share.

### EIA-SRC-02 — Limits on Department of Education control

**Official summary:** [Department of Education — What Is Not Part of ED's Role?](https://www.ed.gov/about/ed-overview/overview-of-us-department-of-education-what-not-part-of-eds-role); [Federal Role FAQ](https://www.ed.gov/about/contact-us/faqs/Elementary%20and%20Secondary%20Education).

**Legal anchor:** Department of Education Organization Act, 20 U.S.C. § 3403(b), as quoted in agency guidance.

**What it supports:** Department officials are not generally authorized to direct school curriculum, instructional content, personnel, or institutional administration, subject to statutory exceptions. The legal text and its exceptions govern.

**Limit:** This should not be misstated as prohibiting every federal educational condition or civil-rights enforcement action.

### EIA-SRC-03 — Elementary and Secondary Education Act / ESSA

**Agency explanation:** [Department of Education — Every Student Succeeds Act](https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/laws-preschool-grade-12-education/esea/what-is-the-every-student-succeeds-act); [ESEA general provisions](https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/laws-preschool-grade-12-education/esea/part-c--general-provisions).

**What it supports:** State assessment, reporting and accountability responsibilities under ESSA, together with limits on federal mandates, directions or control of specific instructional content and standards as a condition of certain funds.

**Limit:** Statutory funding, waiver and oversight requirements must be analyzed section by section; no single source here comprehensively describes all formula grants.

### EIA-SRC-04 — Special education under IDEA

**Official regulations:** [IDEA — About the Department and OSERS](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/about-us-department-of-education/); [IDEA applicability to states and local agencies](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/a/300.2); [IDEA 2026 state determinations](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/idea-files/2026-determination-letters-on-state-implementation-of-idea/).

**What it supports:** Specific protections and obligations for eligible children with disabilities, responsibilities of states and local educational agencies, and federal monitoring roles.

**Limit:** IDEA is not equivalent to a universal constitutional right to every educational service. The applicable scope of federal assistance, program eligibility and legal remedies must be assessed separately.

### EIA-SRC-05 — House legislative education jurisdiction

**Official committee description:** [U.S. House Committee on Education & the Workforce — Jurisdiction](https://edworkforce.house.gov/committee/jurisdiction/); [Early Childhood, Elementary and Secondary Education Subcommittee](https://edworkforce.house.gov/committee/ecese.htm); [Higher Education and Workforce Development Subcommittee](https://edworkforce.house.gov/committee/hewd.htm).

**What it supports:** House committee jurisdiction over education, skills, workforce, special education, and related subjects.

**Limit:** Committee names, subcommittees and membership can change by Congress; jurisdiction is procedural and does not transfer executive functions to committees.

### EIA-SRC-06 — Senate legislative education jurisdiction

**Official committee description:** [Senate HELP Committee — About and Jurisdiction](https://www.help.senate.gov/about).

**What it supports:** HELP Committee jurisdiction over education, training, welfare and certain related activities under Senate rules.

**Limit:** Oversight and legislative consideration are not the same as school operations or appropriations enactment.

### EIA-SRC-07 — Congressional independent oversight

**Independent legislative support:** [GAO — How Does GAO Serve Congress?](https://www.gao.gov/blog/how-does-gao-serve-congress); [GAO, September 2026 K–12 Oversight Findings](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107920).

**What it supports:** GAO's independent oversight and advice for Congress, with current examples concerning ESEA monitoring and chronic absenteeism.

**Limit:** GAO recommendations are not enacted policy; the cited report discusses identifiable 2026 monitoring changes and must not be generalized to all educational oversight.

### EIA-SRC-08 — Department of Education K–12 administration

**Agency functional statement:** [Office of Elementary and Secondary Education](https://www.ed.gov/about/ed-offices/oese).

**What it supports:** Specified federal support, technical assistance and authorized financial assistance associated with elementary and secondary education.

**Limit:** Neither the office nor its programs supersede state/local school governance generally; verify each function in light of time-sensitive interagency arrangements.

### EIA-SRC-09 — Federal research and educational statistics

**Agency research functions:** [Institute of Education Sciences](https://ies.ed.gov/about); [NCES — About](https://ies.ed.gov/national-center-education-statistics-nces/about).

**What it supports:** Federal education research, program evaluation and nonpartisan statistical functions, including NAEP and official educational statistics.

**Limit:** Research organizations document and analyze rather than govern local curricula. The exact status of grants, datasets and reorganized functions requires date-specific verification.

### EIA-SRC-10 — Educational civil-rights enforcement

**Agency enforcement description:** [Department of Education Office for Civil Rights](https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/civil-rights-laws/file-complaint/how-the-office-for-civil-rights-handles-complaints); [DOJ Civil Rights Division Educational Opportunities Section](https://www.justice.gov/crt/educational-opportunities-section).

**Legal anchors:** Title VI, Title IX, Section 504, Title II of the ADA, and other statutes in their respective scope.

**What it supports:** Defined federal administrative and judicial-enforcement responsibilities protecting equal educational access.

**Limit:** The applicable statute, covered recipients, protected class, investigation, remedy, and jurisdiction vary. Neither OCR nor DOJ has general power to set educational curriculum.

### EIA-SRC-11 — Early childhood / Head Start

**Program authority summary:** [Office of Head Start — About](https://www.headstart.gov/about-us/article/about-office-head-start).

**What it supports:** HHS/ACF Office of Head Start grant and oversight functions, carried out through local eligible organizations.

**Limit:** Head Start is a specific program; it neither replaces all early-childhood education nor grants HHS general K–12 curricular authority.

### EIA-SRC-12 — Apprenticeships and workforce learning

**Official program architecture:** [Apprenticeship.gov — The Apprenticeship System](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/about-us/apprenticeship-system).

**What it supports:** Federal Office of Apprenticeship and recognized state apprenticeship agency roles in registering, overseeing and assisting Registered Apprenticeship Programs.

**Limit:** The recognized status and division of authority vary by state or territory and should not be generalized to every vocational program.

### EIA-SRC-13 — Connectivity and learning infrastructure

**Program administration:** [USAC — E-Rate](https://www.usac.org/e-rate/); [USAC — E-Rate Getting Started](https://www.usac.org/e-rate/get-started/).

**What it supports:** Eligible schools and libraries can receive discounts on specified connectivity-related services through a program administered by USAC under FCC oversight.

**Limit:** E-Rate is a telecommunications support program, not a federal curricular or school governance authority.

### EIA-SRC-14 — Tribal and BIE education

**Federal/tribal institutional sources:** [Indian Affairs — BIE Overview](https://www.bia.gov/bie); [BIE — Tribally Controlled Schools](https://www.bie.edu/general-information/tribally-controlled-schools).

**What it supports:** BIE manages federal education functions within its authority; tribally controlled schools operate under tribal control through applicable grants/contracts and are distinct from BIE-operated schools.

**Limit:** The term 'local educational agency' has program-dependent meanings; not all tribally controlled schools are directly operated federal schools or subordinate state LEAs. Tribal sovereignty and relevant treaty/statutory obligations require independent analysis.

### EIA-SRC-15 — 2026 interagency education arrangements

**Agency update:** [Department of Education — Returning Education to the States](https://www.ed.gov/about/initiatives/returning-education-states).

**What it supports:** The Department describes interagency partnerships for administration and coordination of education programs and says authorized funding, program eligibility and applicable protections persist.

**Limit:** The page describes administration policy; every agreement, delegation and possible statutory conflict requires separate inspection. This register does **not** conclude that an executive agreement abolished a congressionally established department or amended congressional statutes.

### EIA-SRC-16 — U.S. territories and program relationships

**Agency explanation:** [Department of Education — 2026 Interagency Arrangements and Territories](https://www.ed.gov/about/initiatives/returning-education-states); [ED Office of Postsecondary Education — Territorial Accreditation Liaison](https://www.ed.gov/laws-and-policy/higher-education-laws-and-policy/college-accreditation/accreditation-postsecondary-education-institutions/accreditation-group-state-liaison-team-roles-duties).

**What it supports:** Guam, American Samoa, Puerto Rico, Northern Mariana Islands and the U.S. Virgin Islands are separately recognized in certain federal education administration contexts.

**Limit:** Inclusion in a federal program does not erase differences among territorial government, citizenship, representation, constitutional status and locally administered education law. A complete territory-by-territory map remains research pending.

### EIA-SRC-17 — Federal judicial jurisdiction

**Judiciary explanation:** [United States Courts — Understanding Federal Courts](https://ord.uscourts.gov/index.php/court-info/about-the-judiciary).

**What it supports:** Article III adjudication within the federal courts' jurisdiction, including constitutional, statutory and other legally cognizable disputes.

**Limit:** Federal courts act on properly presented cases and controversies; they do not administer a national educational curriculum or substitute for ordinary legislative choices.

### EIA-SRC-18 — Education and federal constitutional law

**Decisions:** [San Antonio Independent School District v. Rodriguez, 411 U.S. 1 (1973)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/411/1/); [Plyler v. Doe, 457 U.S. 202 (1982)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/457/202); [Endrew F. v. Douglas County School District, 580 U.S. 386 (2017)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/15-827).

**What it supports:** The Constitution does not generally establish education as a fundamental right under the federal equal-protection analysis in Rodriguez; Plyler prohibits the specific state exclusion of undocumented children from public K–12 education under equal protection; Endrew F. interprets an IDEA educational-benefit standard in its statutory context.

**Limit:** Each case has specific holdings, parties and factual conditions. Distinct state constitutional education guarantees and individual statutory remedies are not comprehensively treated here.

## 6. Open authority questions

1. How do current 2026 interagency agreements change individual administrative points of contact without transferring powers beyond enacted law?
2. For each education program, who bears statutory accountability, who operates it today, and who responds to complaints and appeals?
3. Which powers and guarantees are established under each state constitution, education code and applicable court precedent?
4. What is the correct school-government hierarchy for each U.S. territory, and where do program eligibility rules diverge?
5. How do tribal education authorities, BIE-operated schools and tribally controlled schools differ in practice and legal accountability?
6. What legal routes govern federal/state educational data sharing, privacy, longitudinal research and authorized use of student data?
7. Which benefits from federal education coordination can be demonstrated, and which risks arise from duplicative reporting or overly centralized control?
8. Which institutions, if any, should support educational quality outside compulsory schooling, including adult informal and independent learning?
9. How should federal courts and state courts be represented as safeguards without treating them as policymaking branches of education administration?
10. How can public experience, stakeholder concerns and independent statistics be combined without substituting one for the other?

## 7. Editorial disposition

This file records selected grounded authorities. It does **not** certify that any existing office is universally effective or ineffective, that a contemplated future contribution has been authorized, or that an agency restructuring is necessary.

Before publishing specific legal or budget proposals, build program-specific authority records with legal text, delegation, fiscal source, administrative owner, remedies, temporal status and responsible reviewer. Any legal conclusion with material consequences needs qualified independent review.

**Review request:** Congressional procedural specialist; federal/state education law; IDEA and civil rights; tribal and territorial education; curriculum/family rights; data protection and statistical methods.
