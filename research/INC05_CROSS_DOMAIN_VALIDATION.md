## 1. Research question

Can the same future-state-first manuscript standard describe human services, economic activity and digital security without becoming a current-state audit, a party platform or a prescriptive government implementation plan?

**Initial finding: yes, provisionally.** The shared nine-question chapter structure organizes these different subjects while preserving their particular legal and ethical tensions. The three chapters require different outcome measures, but they can share a traceability pattern: foundational values → human outcome → enabling capability → conceptually possible pathways → conditions for progress → safeguards → lawful institutional relationships → public research questions.

The evidence base is **bounded**. This evaluation demonstrates editorial and structural consistency, not a validation of proposed social guarantees, causal policy effects, constitutional reform, or public consensus.

### Architectural ownership rule

A shared capability such as **privacy-preserving identity, regional service continuity or public evidence quality** should eventually be modeled once in the architecture repository, with links from multiple chapters. A domain chapter describes what the capability makes possible for people; an institution profile states an actor's lawful responsibilities; the architecture model records canonical interfaces and dependencies.

Do not claim the existing manuscript has already completed that canonical mapping. The table is an **integration proposal for later research**.

## 4. Scenario tests of the chapter standard

### Scenario A — A rural region loses both telecommunications and emergency power

A cyber event affecting a communications provider can interrupt healthcare coordination, business payments, emergency contact and resident access to government services simultaneously.

**What the chapter standard captures:** People-centered health outcomes and continuity; economic harm to households and businesses; cyber resilience and recovery; infrastructure dependencies; state/local responsibilities and private ownership; risks from poorly designed identity or emergency data sharing.

**What it does not yet support:** A verified risk probability, engineering design, deployment timeline, appropriation, or specific lawful data-sharing order. Those belong to source-backed research and later authorized implementation dossiers.

### Scenario B — Healthcare spending decreases, but patients wait longer

Reducing spending is a useful fiscal possibility only if its real consequences are understood. The health chapter's HLT-K01 access and HLT-K02 avoidable-burden measures must be interpreted with HLT-K03 household cost and quality guardrails.

**Failure identified:** A single financial target could reward rationing, omitted clinical need or cost shifting.

**Provisional response:** Retain multiple independent outcome checks and assess whether quality and fair access actually improve. Do not adopt an automatic ownership or financing conclusion.

### Scenario C — A digital identity system reduces account fraud but excludes people without compatible devices

A promising cyber control may improve CYB-K03 for participants while burdening elderly users, people with disabilities, lower-income households and other people unable to use the chosen authentication mechanism.

**Failure identified:** A favorable security metric can conceal unequal access and intrude on privacy.

**Provisional response:** Pair cyber integrity with meaningful alternatives, rights, data minimization and redress; link to health and economic access outcomes. No mandatory universal biometric identity is inferred.

### Scenario D — Productivity rises while workforce advancement stalls

Advanced automation may increase national production and enterprise return while wages or local opportunities stagnate for some groups.

**Failure identified:** Aggregate growth and sector output alone fail to show durable opportunity or the costs of transition.

**Provisional response:** Use EPR-K01 real household capacity and EPR-K02 sustained mobility alongside EPR-K03 productive value, and examine education, community resilience and labor adaptation without committing to a particular policy.

### Scenario E — Cyber threat attribution becomes a mechanism for excluding lawful views

Transparency around documented covert direction can aid public judgment. But a classifier of undesirable opinion could be misused as a government censorship mechanism or a biased private access decision.

**Failure identified:** Claims of 'information integrity' can conflict with FP-02, FP-04, FP-14 and public dissent if evidence and authority are weak.

**Provisional response:** Define information integrity as evidence/provenance and security of identity, not compulsory viewpoint agreement; require lawful scope, documented attribution, independent review and correction.

## 5. Recommended measurement standard improvements

The existing chapter template calls for an outcome table but leaves the relationship between high-level outcomes, indicators and adverse consequences underspecified. The pilots support a modest amendment for a **future PR**, not an unreviewed rewrite of the accepted template.

1. Each substantial domain should propose **one to three headline outcome measures** aligned with its core public question, unless evidence genuinely requires fewer or more.
2. Distinguish **outcome**, **diagnostic/driver**, and **guardrail** measures. Do not use driver counts (funds spent, tools installed, training certificates, report volumes) as proof of benefit.
3. Each candidate measure should state **population and definition**, intended evidence source, required disaggregation, susceptibility to gaming, and at least one countervailing harm measure.
4. Set numeric targets only when an accepted source baseline, method, comparator and confidence are available; no target is required for a conceptual draft.
5. Record legal authority and data minimization before creating instruments that collect personal information or monitor civic behavior.
6. Separate **measure selection** from **recommendation approval**. Showing that an indicator is useful does not establish that a proposed intervention changes it.

These refinements align with the [current authoring standard](../docs/AUTHORING_AND_REVIEW_STANDARD.md) and can be added after public review.

## 6. Editorial weaknesses and corrective actions

| Finding | Severity | Recommendation | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chapter size can become burdensome for public readers. | Moderate | Keep a short public-facing summary and collapsible/detailed evidence in the web presentation; do not remove substantive prose without editorial review. | Proposed |
| National headline data can falsely imply local representativeness. | High | Require geography/population disaggregation and evidence caveats beside each statistic. | Included in drafts; specialist validation pending |
| Future-state language may imply an enacted entitlement. | High | Distinguish law from proposed rights and outcome objectives. | Included; legal review pending |
| Institutions can be listed as if responsible for a whole national outcome. | High | Use the role/authority distinction demonstrated in INC-04; cross-domain office-by-office authority mapping remains later work. | Included conceptually |
| Tax, ownership and surveillance alternatives can be mistaken for adopted reforms. | High | Mark each as an open decision with counterarguments and legal boundaries. | Included; public review pending |

| Rights and jurisdiction vary by context. | High | Request legal and affected-community reviews before any binding recommendation. | Pending |

## 9. Recommended next incremental work

**INC-06 — Branch/Department/Office Institutional Reference Structure** should remain the planned next major workstream, but two prerequisites emerge:

- **Measurement specification v0.1.0:** Give each selected headline outcome a precise denominator, population, guardrail, candidate source and feasibility grade before discussing numeric targets.
- **Shared capability deduplication:** Map resilience, secure data exchange, workforce development, public evidence and financing once in the architecture model, allowing all domains to reference them.

Neither prerequisite requires making this manuscript into a detailed current-state audit. They can be delivered as linked research increments with public review while the plan's narrative remains conceptual.

*INC-05 documents a research and authoring method that can scale across national domains. It does not certify that any future outcome, financing model, or security policy has been independently proven.*
