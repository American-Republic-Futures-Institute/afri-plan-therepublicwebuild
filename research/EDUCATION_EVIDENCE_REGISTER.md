## Scope and interpretation

This register records selected **verifiable present and historical observations**, not a comprehensive current-state assessment of American education. Evidence is included to establish an honest baseline for future-state inquiry. The main manuscript focuses on what future conditions should be achieved; the research file retains the supporting methodological details.

The following distinctions are mandatory:

- Findings on specific assessments are not comprehensive judgments about an educational system.
- **NAEP Proficient is not the same as grade-level proficiency** under state or local definitions. Neither main NAEP nor long-term trend NAEP is a simple score for all learning.
- Age-based long-term trend assessments and grade-based main NAEP assessments use different designs; do not concatenate or directly equate results.
- PIAAC adult competencies do not isolate the causal effects of any one schooling practice, program, or administration.
- A nationally relevant education outcome does not, by itself, supply federal legal authority to direct school curricula.
- Aspirational rights or services described by ARFI are not automatically existing enforceable federal rights.

## EDU-SRC-01: 2024 Main NAEP Reading

**Source:** National Center for Education Statistics, *The Nation's Report Card*, 2024 Reading Assessment, Grades 4 and 8.  
**Official URL:** https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/reports/reading/2024/g4_8/  
**Assessment period:** January–March 2024  
**Claim class:** FACT (reported national sampled assessment)

**Supported findings relevant to the chapter:**

- The national grade 4 average reading score in 2024 was **5 points lower than in 2019** and **2 points lower than in 2022**.
- **31 percent of grade 4 students** reached or exceeded the NAEP Proficient level on the 2024 assessment.
- NAEP's own explanation states that NAEP Proficient **does not correspond to grade-level proficiency established by state or local tests**.
- The report includes state, district, student-group and percentile-level information that can inform later disaggregated research.

**Interpretation boundaries:** The 31% statistic must not be restated as “only 31% of fourth graders can read at grade level.” The assessment does not comprehensively evaluate science, civic reasoning, writing, creativity, vocational learning or lifetime opportunity. A change in scores alone does not establish the cause.

**Future research:** Compare different populations, students with disabilities and English learners, changes over time, cohort effects, instructional methods, participation and potential measurement artifacts.

## EDU-SRC-02: 2025 NAEP Long-Term Trend

**Source:** National Center for Education Statistics, *2025 Long-Term Trend Reading and Mathematics Assessments*, ages 9 and 13.  
**Official URL:** https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/ltt/2025/  
**Publication:** June 10, 2026 (see official report archive: https://www.nationsreportcard.gov/report_archive.aspx)  
**Claim class:** FACT (reported national age-based sampled assessment)

**Supported findings relevant to the chapter:**

- For **nine-year-olds**, national average reading and mathematics scores in 2025 were each **4 points higher than in 2022**.
- For **thirteen-year-olds**, neither reading nor mathematics average scores changed significantly in 2025 compared with 2023.
- The 2025 results for thirteen-year-olds remained below the pre-pandemic results in 2020.
- The report specifically distinguishes its long-term trend assessments from the main NAEP assessment series.

**Interpretation boundaries:** A recovery in one tested population and period cannot be generalized into a complete national educational recovery. It also does not prove a particular instructional or funding policy succeeded. The age-based and grade-based instruments are not interchangeable.

**Future research:** Explore learning at different ages, distributions and timeframes; examine assessment designs, socioeconomic conditions, schooling patterns and independent evidence.

## EDU-SRC-03: 2023 Adult Skills PIAAC

**Source:** National Center for Education Statistics, *Highlights of U.S. National Results, 2023 Program for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies (PIAAC)*.  
**Official URL:** https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/piaac/2023/national_results.asp  
**Fieldwork:** August 2022–June 2023  
**Population:** U.S. adults ages 16–65, with study-specific eligibility and sampling  
**Claim class:** FACT (survey/assessment result, method cautions)

**Supported findings relevant to the chapter:**

- In 2023, **28%** of assessed U.S. adults were in the lowest literacy proficiency category (Level 1 or below), compared with **19%** in 2017.
- **34%** were in the lowest numeracy category in 2023, compared with **29%** in 2017.
- NCES reports that average adult literacy and numeracy scores declined between 2017 and 2023.
- **Response rates were relatively low**, including for the United States and several other participating countries. The study's technical notes should be consulted when using point estimates and comparisons.

**Interpretation boundaries:** The results do not establish whether the cause lies in childhood schools, changes in adult populations, skill retention, labor-market arrangements, differential response or other influences. Do not treat numeracy and literacy scores as the sole dimensions of adult competence.

**Future research:** Validate subgroup evidence, confidence intervals, low response implications, pathways for adult literacy/numeracy and interaction with employment and lifelong learning access.

## EDU-SRC-04: Federal, State, and Local Roles

**Source:** U.S. Department of Education, *Federal Role in Education*.  
**Official URL:** https://www.ed.gov/about/ed-overview/federal-role-in-education?lang=en  
**Claim class:** LAW / INSTITUTIONAL (agency summary of governmental roles; verify statute before specific conclusions)

**Supported summary:** Education is predominantly a **state and local responsibility** in the United States. States and communities, along with public and private education organizations, traditionally establish schools, curricula, and enrollment and graduation requirements. Federal responsibilities and programs have distinct statutory and constitutional bases.

**Limitations:** The page includes older financial figures from school year 2012–13. **Do not use those as contemporary federal spending shares.** The webpage is a general explanatory source, not a substitute for controlling constitutional and statutory provisions about a specific proposed federal activity.

**Future research:** Develop separate legal authority records for state/local education responsibilities; federal education funding, research and civil-rights obligations; tribal education; territorial education; and variations among home, private, public and vocational settings.

## EDU-SRC-05: Individuals with Disabilities Education Act

**Source:** U.S. Department of Education, Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), including its official regulations and statutory purpose.

- https://sites.ed.gov/idea/about-idea/
- https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/a/300.1
- https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/a/300.17
- https://sites.ed.gov/idea/statute-chapter-33/subchapter-i/1400/d

**Claim class:** LAW (controlling scope must be researched for each application)

**Supported summary:** IDEA makes a **free appropriate public education** available to eligible children with disabilities through legally defined duties, special education and related services. It includes protections for children and parents and responsibilities for states and other authorized entities.

**Interpretation boundary:** This specific statutory guarantee should not be conflated with an unqualified existing general federal constitutional entitlement to every form of public or private education. IDEA's age scope, eligibility, required procedures, parental rights and jurisdictional treatment require source-specific review.

**Future research:** Examine implementation quality, accessibility, rural specialist access, transition services, family recourse and outcomes, without presuming that statutory duties alone guarantee satisfactory real-world experience.

## Research integrity and update procedure

Before revising a factual claim, identify the source, assessment population, data collection period, publication date and interpretation caveat. For later publications, add newer comparable evidence rather than replacing old results without preserving their historical role.

**Current gap:** This register is not yet an exhaustive education evidence base. It does not independently validate a universal definition of quality, causal efficacy of future options, educational costs and benefits across jurisdictions, or the legal requirements of every pathway. These gaps remain explicit research work, not editorial defects to conceal.
