## Evidence method and limits

This register supports the future-state chapters for [Health (DOM-03)](../manuscript/domains/DOM-03-health-and-human-well-being.md), [Economy (DOM-05)](../manuscript/domains/DOM-05-economy-work-and-prosperity.md), and [Cybersecurity, Privacy and Information Integrity (DOM-12)](../manuscript/domains/DOM-12-cybersecurity-privacy-and-information-integrity.md).

Sources are used for three distinct purposes: a **bounded observation** about the current situation; a **description of existing legal or institutional authority**; or a **methodological reference** for potential outcome measurement. They do not independently endorse any ARFI future-state concept, prove a causal policy theory, or imply that every public institution is deficient.

The main manuscript deliberately remains weighted toward what future conditions should become possible. This register retains primary source URLs and qualifications so research can be updated without rewriting the ideals to match the latest figures.

## Health and Human Well-Being (HLT)

### HLT-SRC-01 — Final 2024 mortality and longevity data

**Publisher:** CDC National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS)  
**Title:** [Mortality in the United States, 2024 — NCHS Data Brief 548](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db548.htm)  
**Publication:** January 2026; final mortality data for calendar year 2024  
**Classification:** FACT / population outcome measurement

The NCHS reported **life expectancy at birth of 79.0 years for 2024**, up 0.6 year from 2023. The age-adjusted death rate declined 3.8% from 2023 to 2024.

**Limitations:** Life expectancy is a summary mortality measure, not an individual lifespan prediction or a complete measure of healthcare quality. The reported change is not attributable to one financing, insurance, prevention or clinical-care program without causal analysis. Investigate disparities and cause-specific differences in later research.

### HLT-SRC-02 — Health spending and financing categories

**Publisher:** Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS)  
**Title:** [National Health Expenditure (NHE) Fact Sheet](https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet)  
**Period:** 2024 historical estimates, published in 2026  
**Classification:** FACT / expenditure accounting

CMS estimates **$5.3 trillion in national health expenditures in 2024**, approximately **18.0% of GDP**. Spending categories cover services and goods, administration, public health and associated activities.

**Limitations:** Total spending and share of GDP do not quantify quality, health improvement, affordability for a particular household, medical need, continuity, clinical safety or value. Projection series must be labeled as forecasts rather than observed facts.

### HLT-SRC-03 — Health insurance coverage is not healthcare access

**Publisher:** U.S. Census Bureau  
**Title:** [Income, Poverty and Health Insurance Coverage in the United States: 2025](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/income-poverty-health-insurance-coverage.html)  
**Publication:** September 15, 2026 (2025 income/coverage data collected in 2026 CPS ASEC)  
**Classification:** FACT / coverage survey

The Census Bureau reported **26.7 million people, or 7.9%, uninsured for the entire year in 2025**, with no statistically significant change compared with 2024 on that measure.

**Limitations:** 'Uninsured for the entire year' is a specific annual CPS ASEC measure, not the share uninsured at an instant, nor a direct measure of actual timely medical access or clinical outcomes. Different surveys and estimates of coverage must not be combined without aligning definitions.

### HLT-SRC-04 — Health workforce shortage designations

**Publisher:** Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA)  
**Title:** [Health Workforce Shortage Areas Dashboard](https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-workforce/shortage-areas/dashboard)  
**Data review:** August 27, 2026 (dashboard periodically updated)  
**Classification:** FACT / administrative designation

HRSA reports that **about 20% of the U.S. population** resides in designated primary medical-care Health Professional Shortage Areas. The dashboard distinguishes primary care, dental and mental-health shortage categories and rural, urban, population-specific and facility-based designations.

**Limitations:** An administrative shortage-area designation does not directly measure how long a specific person waits for an appointment, service quality, insurance acceptance, travel burden, or all clinician workforce deficits. Use official methods and timestamp.

### HLT-SRC-05 — Medicaid federal-state responsibilities

**Publisher:** CMS / Medicaid.gov  
**Title:** [Medicaid program and its administration](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid)  
**Classification:** LAW / official program description

Medicaid is jointly funded by the federal government and states and administered by states under federal requirements. Eligibility, benefits, financing and administration include specific federal minimums and state choices.

**Limitations:** The lawful scope differs among Medicaid, Medicare, CHIP, other federal programs and state-based arrangements. This source is not a generalized federal power to direct all healthcare delivery.

### HLT-SRC-06 — Cross-program coordination without presumed ownership model

**Publisher:** CMS  
**Title:** [Medicare-Medicaid Coordination Office](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/medicaid-coordination/about)  
**Classification:** INSTITUTIONAL / official function

CMS identifies specific responsibilities for improving coordination for people eligible for both Medicare and Medicaid, in collaboration with states and other partners.

**Limitations:** The existence of such a function does not prove that public or private ownership is best for all healthcare, or that a national system can be reorganized through administrative agreement alone.

## Economy, Work, and Prosperity (ECO)

### ECO-SRC-01 — 2025 incomes and distribution

**Publisher:** U.S. Census Bureau  
**Title:** [Income in the United States: 2025 (P60-289)](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/2026/demo/income-poverty/p60-289.html)  
**Publication:** September 2026; CPS ASEC  
**Classification:** FACT / household survey

Census reports **real median household income of $87,460 in 2025**, **up 2.6%** from its **revised 2024 estimate of $85,210**. The income Gini index was not statistically different in 2025 versus 2024; the 90th percentile estimate rose and the 10th percentile did not change significantly.

**Limitations:** Income here is not a comprehensive accounting of assets, debt, non-cash support, service quality or opportunity; the report contains pretax and post-tax definitions that must not be interchanged. Its revisions mean older contemporaneous 2024 publications may report different values.

### ECO-SRC-02 — Poverty is sensitive to measurement

**Publisher:** U.S. Census Bureau  
**Title:** [Income, Poverty and Health Insurance Coverage in the United States: 2025](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026/income-poverty-health-insurance-coverage.html)  
**Publication:** September 15, 2026  
**Classification:** FACT / household survey

The **official poverty rate was 10.2% in 2025**; the **Supplemental Poverty Measure (SPM) rate was 13.1%**, not statistically different from 2024. These use distinct income/resource and need accounting.

**Limitations:** Neither rate by itself describes all dimensions of material security, mobility, housing conditions, family structure or disability-related needs. The measures must not be collapsed into a single figure without explaining the definitions.

### ECO-SRC-03 — GDP measures production

**Publisher:** U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA)  
**Title:** [The Expenditures Approach to Measuring GDP](https://bea.gov/index.php/news/blog/2025-06-03/expenditures-approach-measuring-gdp)  
**Publication:** June 3, 2025  
**Classification:** METHOD

GDP measures the value of domestic goods and services produced, using multiple accounting approaches. It is valuable national economic information but not a complete measure of product durability, household well-being, ecological effects or fairness.

**Limitations:** Additional well-being metrics should complement GDP rather than falsely claim GDP is a measure of social welfare or eliminate its legitimate macroeconomic uses.

### ECO-SRC-04 — Existing competition-law boundaries

**Publisher:** Federal Trade Commission  
**Title:** [Monopolization Defined](https://www.ftc.gov/advice-guidance/competition-guidance/guide-antitrust-laws/single-firm-conduct/monopolization-defined)  
**Classification:** LAW / agency legal explanation

The FTC distinguishes lawful success through superior products or management from improper acquisition or maintenance of market power, and explains competition effects and possible defenses.

**Limitations:** Neither a large market share nor ownership by a private-equity firm is in itself proof of unlawful monopolization. Specific enforcement claims require market definition, evidence and applicable legal standards.

### ECO-SRC-05 — Federal fiscal sustainability

**Publisher:** Government Accountability Office  
**Title:** [The Nation's Fiscal Health: Urgent and Sustained Action Needed](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108610)  
**Publication:** June 11, 2026  
**Classification:** FACT / official fiscal analysis and projection

GAO reported publicly held debt of approximately **$31.3 trillion as of April 2026**, near the size of the economy, and projected a rising debt-to-GDP path under continued policies.

**Limitations:** 'Debt held by the public' differs from gross federal debt. Long-term ratios are conditional projections, not precise forecasts or proof that a balanced budget is required every year. Debt sustainability must consider economic growth, interest costs, public investment and risks.

### ECO-SRC-06 — Monetary policy has multiple instruments

**Publisher:** Federal Reserve Board  
**Title:** [Monetary Policy — policy tools and operations](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy.htm)  
**Site reviewed:** October 2026  
**Classification:** LAW / INSTITUTIONAL / METHOD

The Federal Reserve describes open-market operations, interest on reserve balances, discount-window lending, repurchase facilities, and other monetary and liquidity instruments.

**Limitations:** Monetary operations, Treasury debt issuance and fiscal appropriations are not interchangeable authorities. Creating reserve balances to purchase securities does not erase the underlying economic obligations. New monetary mechanisms require careful legal and macroeconomic scrutiny.

### ECO-SRC-07 — Asset ownership and wealth data need separate accounting

**Publisher:** U.S. Census Bureau  
**Title:** [Income and Household Asset/Debt Data](https://www.census.gov/topics/income-poverty/income.html)  
**Period:** 2024 SIPP asset and debt tables released in July 2026  
**Classification:** METHOD / DATA SOURCE

Census publishes separate information on household income and on assets, ownership and debt.

**Limitations:** Income and net wealth must not be treated as the same taxable or distributional base. Estate/trust law, state property taxation and federal income-tax law are distinct subjects; this citation does not establish the constitutionality or desirability of any proposed wealth tax.

## Cybersecurity, Privacy, and Information Integrity (CYB)

### CYB-SRC-01 — Outcome-based cybersecurity framework

**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)  
**Title:** [Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20)  
**Published:** February 26, 2024  
**Classification:** METHOD

NIST defines six cybersecurity risk-management functions: **Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover**. The Framework is outcome-oriented, intended for organizations across sectors and sizes, and does not prescribe a specific technology or architecture.

**Limitations:** CSF use is not automatically a legal compliance determination, a measure of all human harms, or evidence that a particular control prevents a specific incident. Government and private entities have different mandatory obligations.

### CYB-SRC-02 — Privacy risk framework

**Publisher:** NIST  
**Title:** [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework)  
**Classification:** METHOD

NIST's Privacy Framework is a voluntary method for identifying and managing privacy risks while creating beneficial products and services. As of October 2026, the site includes **Privacy Framework 1.1 Initial Public Draft** materials; draft revisions must not be represented as universally adopted binding law.

**Limitations:** Privacy risk is not exhausted by information-security controls or incidents. Individual autonomy, legitimate purpose, collection minimization, power asymmetry, recourse and unexpected data uses matter.

### CYB-SRC-03 — Federal-agency information rights and boundaries

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Justice, Office of Privacy and Civil Liberties  
**Title:** [Privacy Act of 1974 overview](https://www.justice.gov/opcl/privacy-act-1974)  
**Updated:** June 2026  
**Classification:** LAW

The Privacy Act, **5 U.S.C. § 552a**, places requirements on federal agency systems of records about identifiable people, including collection, use, dissemination and various access and amendment provisions, with statutory exceptions.

**Limitations:** It does **not** automatically govern every private company, every U.S. personal-data record, or every government entity in precisely the same way. Each proposed data-sharing power needs its own controlling authority and exceptions review.

### CYB-SRC-04 — Civilian technical assistance and federalism

**Publisher:** Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)  
**Title:** [Security Advisors](https://www.cisa.gov/about/regions/security-advisors)  
**Classification:** INSTITUTIONAL / official service description

CISA's regional Cyber Security Advisors offer assistance and liaison services to critical-infrastructure owners and operators and state, local, tribal and territorial officials.

**Limitations:** A description of advisory service is not generalized authority to demand access to private networks, conduct domestic military operations or collect unrestricted personal data. Consent and lawful program scope must be analyzed.

### CYB-SRC-05 — Civilian voluntary cybersecurity performance goals

**Publisher:** CISA  
**Title:** [Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals](https://www.cisa.gov/cybersecurity-performance-goals)  
**Classification:** METHOD / institutional guidance

CISA describes baseline cybersecurity practices and related sector-specific goals built around standards and practical risk reduction.

**Limitations:** Baseline goals are neither a claim of full security nor an automatic regulatory command for every private or public entity. The CISA materials may evolve.

### CYB-SRC-06 — 2026 ransomware resilience model

**Publisher:** NIST  
**Title:** [IR 8374 Revision 1 — Ransomware Risk Management](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8374/r1/final)  
**Published:** June 2026  
**Classification:** METHOD / threat and control reference

NIST describes ransomware as disrupting access and potentially exfiltrating data, and links prevention, incident response and recovery outcomes to CSF 2.0.

**Limitations:** Recommended security outcomes do not prove any one organization is secure; evidence of actual continuity and recovery is necessary.

### CYB-SRC-07 — Foreign influence disclosure versus viewpoint restriction

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Justice  
**Title:** [Foreign Agents Registration Act — FAQ](https://www.justice.gov/nsd-fara/frequently-asked-questions)  
**Updated:** July 2026  
**Classification:** LAW / agency explanation

DOJ explains that FARA establishes certain registration and disclosure duties for covered agents of foreign principals, and states that the law does not regulate the **content** of protected speech or messaging.

**Limitations:** The coverage of FARA has defined statutory and regulatory criteria. It is not a blanket ban on foreign viewpoints or an authority for governmental determination of permissible political opinions.

## Cross-domain research boundaries

| Question | Why the existing sources do not settle it | Required next work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does more spending create better health outcomes? | CMS expenditures and CDC mortality are not causal comparisons. | Risk-adjusted health outcomes, access, quality and financing counterfactuals. |
| Does stronger growth mean broader prosperity? | GDP and median income do not capture all assets, mobility, externalities and local burdens. | Household panels, wealth/debt, costs, product quality and environmental evidence. |
| Does security equal safety for people? | Framework adoption and reported incident counts can hide lost service and intrusive data collection. | Verified recovery, harm to people, privacy complaints, false positives and remedies. |
| Can federal authority implement any preferred national outcome? | Legal powers and duties differ among branches, states, tribes, territories and private partners. | Specific institutional authority crosswalks after conceptual chapters are reviewed. |
| Which delivery or ownership model is correct? | One metric cannot establish public vs private superiority across sectors. | Comparative performance, cost, choice, feasibility, rights and opportunity costs. |

## Source maintenance

All observations should retain their **source period**, method and limitations. Update source records when new official releases appear; preserve old observations as historical evidence rather than silently replacing figures. Synthesize public experiences with independent research without treating a complaint sample as a representative survey.

**Next research:** validate primary law for specific reforms, operationalize 1–3 headline outcomes per domain with properly defined populations, measurement windows, risk adjustment and independently observable guardrails. Until that work is complete, numeric targets and legal conclusions remain open.

*These sources support a bounded preliminary inquiry, not a comprehensive causal evaluation or government program audit.*
