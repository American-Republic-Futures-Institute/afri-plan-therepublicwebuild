# Federal Executive Education Functions — Future-Role Profile

**The Republic We Build | ARFI**  
**Profile ID:** EDU-INST-EXE-01 | **Version:** 0.1.0 — conceptual draft  
**Level:** Executive branch; relevant Cabinet offices, agencies and authorized programs  
**Reference date:** October 16, 2026 | **Status:** Open to legal, administrative and public review

> **Public purpose.** Federal executive institutions can support education by administering laws, financing authorized programs, producing credible evidence and enforcing legal protections. Those contributions have distinct legal bases and are not a national command over schools or curricula.

## 1. Institutional Identity and Present Lawful Role

The **Department of Education**, established by statute, performs functions authorized by Congress. Under 20 U.S.C. § 3403(b), Department officials lack general authority to control schools' curriculum, instruction, personnel and administration, except where law authorizes particular actions. The Elementary and Secondary Education Act/ESSA contains further limits on federal direction of instructional content and standards as a condition of funding. [EIA-SRC-01–03](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-01-federalstatelocal-education-relationship)

## 2. Relationship to the Republic We Seek

Federal education functions can contribute to better understanding of learning quality (EDU-O01–O04 and EDU-O10), appropriate aid and access (EDU-O06, EDU-O09), learner protections (EDU-O06–O07), and bridges to adult/technical pathways (EDU-O05 and EDU-O08).

The central future concept is *effective support within lawful boundaries*, not maximization of federal administrative reach. Different programs may legitimately rely on grants, evidence, enforcement, technical assistance or direct federal provision for limited authorized settings. No one delivery mechanism is adopted.

## 3. Capabilities and Relationships

**Credible public evidence:** Independent statistical functions can help distinguish genuine learning from compliance proxies, support state and local comparisons within methodological limits, and disseminate research in understandable terms. Source quality and statistical privacy remain essential.

**Focused and lawful support:** Statutory grants and services should be evaluated for accessible participation, quality, eligibility clarity and unintended burdens on local providers. Federal support may fill identifiable gaps without overriding state/local school governance.

**Rights accountability:** Executive enforcement bodies may investigate and act under specified laws; protected individuals must retain fair procedures and access to remedies. Agency enforcement does not substitute for independent courts.

**Connected lifelong pathways:** Authorized workforce, postsecondary and early-childhood programs can contribute to continuity where their statutory duties permit. Their coordination must not erase program-specific legal safeguards or improperly share learner information.

**Privacy and security:** Education records, assessments, eligibility data and learner support information require constrained access and responsible processing. Security and automation cannot be treated as independent authority to surveil learners.

## 4. Accountability, Rights, and Boundaries

- Any federal assistance is bounded by its statute, appropriation, conditions and jurisdiction.
- Statutory prohibitions on federal curricular control remain relevant even when national outcomes are compelling.
- Civil-rights and disability obligations must be enforced through their actual legal procedures, not a vague national promise.
- Independent statistics cannot be reshaped to support a preferred reform narrative.
- Student data protections and institutional transparency must coexist with credible audit and appeal.
- Federal program coordination must be reviewed for lawfulness and public value, including effects on states, territorial bodies and tribal governments.
- Administrative arrangements evolving during 2026 should be documented by specific agreement, delegation, effective date and controlling law.

## 5. Concepts for Future Evolution

Possible future improvements include more usable public evidence, clearer federal program service delivery, stronger coordination where legally permitted, proportionate and reliable oversight, and simplification of duplicative requirements.

We should also be willing to preserve a presently functioning program, shift an administrative process within lawful authority, or consider statutory reallocation **only after comparing options**. The conceptual goal is learning and citizen benefit, not a predetermined model of centralization or fragmentation.

## 6. Implications for People and Institutions

Learners and families should know what each program can provide and how to obtain help or contest a decision. States, schools and tribal/territorial authorities should be able to identify federal responsibilities without uncertainty about which institution administers an authorized benefit.

Executive coordination has to respect Congress's lawmaking/appropriations functions and courts' adjudicative independence. Community and commercial partners remain autonomous unless a valid statute or agreement imposes specific duties.

## 7. Questions and Evidence Needed

1. What is the exact legal program authority and current administrative point of contact after the 2026 interagency agreements?
2. Which data and evaluation functions produce useful evidence while meeting privacy and statistical confidentiality requirements?
3. Which K–12 grants and higher-education assistance arrangements demonstrably improve access or learning quality?
4. Where do state and local educators experience genuinely unnecessary federal reporting burdens?
5. How should IDEA oversight maintain both legal compliance and real educational progress?
6. Can authorized federal assistance promote lifelong pathways without privileging one provider model?
7. What protections are required before administrative AI or digital educational tools are used?
8. Which existing service methods work effectively and should remain stable?
