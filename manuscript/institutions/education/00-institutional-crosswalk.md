> **Purpose.** The education vision belongs to the people who learn, teach, raise families, and build communities. Government institutions can help make that vision possible, but no single branch or office controls the entire educational ecosystem. The central inquiry is what capabilities and lawful relationships might enable effective learning—not which department should be ordered to deliver a predetermined policy.

## 1. Begin with the outcome, not the organization chart

The [education future-state chapter](../../02-education-and-lifelong-learning.md) sets out ten candidate national outcomes, twelve enabling capabilities, and eight conditions for sustained progress. Those conditions do not imply that education is a federally operated service, that every community should adopt identical methods, or that a national research institute can impose its conclusions.

The federal institutional view has a different purpose. It asks what Congress may legitimately consider, what executive agencies presently administer, what the courts are empowered to decide, how states and local institutions exercise their primary educational responsibilities, and how tribal and territorial relationships must be represented accurately.

This is therefore a **many-to-many relationship model**, not an executive-chain-of-command diagram. Outcomes and capabilities may depend on several jurisdictions, nongovernmental partners and rights-holders. Each connection below is a candidate contribution for study, **not a prescriptive duty assignment**.

The constitutional and legal distinctions matter. The Department of Education's own description recognizes education as primarily a state and local responsibility. Federal officials generally lack authority to direct curricula and instructional content except where law specifically provides otherwise. Congress, executive departments, courts, states, tribal governments and territorial authorities have distinct powers and obligations. [Authority sources: EIA-SRC-01–03](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-01-federalstatelocal-education-relationship)

## 2. Institutional overview in plain language

### The legislative branch: defining and supervising lawful federal commitments

Congress may enact legislation, determine appropriations and conduct oversight within constitutional limitations. The House Committee on Education & the Workforce and the Senate HELP Committee are principal education-related forums under chamber rules; appropriations and other committees participate where their jurisdictions require. GAO supplies independent investigation and analysis to inform Congress. [EIA-SRC-05–07](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-05-house-legislative-education-jurisdiction)

A legislature's role in future education is not simply to increase or reduce a budget. It may need to ask how existing protections function, how financing requirements affect communities, whether program measures reflect genuine learning, and whether legally bounded public investments can help students and adults. Questions, evidence and public experience should inform those choices. None of these functions grants a committee power to directly manage local schools.

See the [legislative education profile](01-legislative-education-functions.md).

### The executive branch: administering specific public responsibilities

Federal executive contributions are distributed among authorized education, research, disability, civil-rights, early-childhood, workforce, tribal education and connectivity programs. Program administration is not the same as a comprehensive power over education. Within enacted limits, agencies may provide financial assistance, gather evidence, enforce requirements, enable voluntary partnerships and help authorized recipients navigate programs.

The administration of some functions can change. For example, federal education agencies have described interagency arrangements in 2026. A change in who performs an administrative task is not proof that Congress amended the underlying statutory duty. [EIA-SRC-15](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-15-2026-interagency-education-arrangements)

See the [federal executive education profile](02-federal-executive-education-functions.md).

### The judicial branch: independent enforcement of law, not education administration

Courts address cases within jurisdiction, including disputes about constitutional protections, discrimination, statutory education entitlements, contractual claims and lawful administrative conduct. Their role is to resolve legal controversies and provide appropriate remedies, not to decide which education innovation is preferable or establish a nationwide curriculum. Federal and state courts may apply different sources of law in different circumstances.

See the [courts and legal remedies profile](03-education-courts-and-remedies.md).

### States, localities, tribal nations and territories: distinct educational responsibility

State and local authorities are central to primary and secondary schooling. Tribal nations and the Bureau of Indian Education operate within legally distinctive arrangements that cannot be reduced to ordinary state subdivisions. U.S. territories have their own governance relationships and cannot be treated as interchangeable. Public and private providers, families and communities also make significant contributions that are not synonymous with delegated governmental authority.

See the [state, local, tribal and territorial profile](04-education-intergovernmental-relationships.md).

## 5. Three examples of connected institutional questions

### 5.1 Foundational literacy without federal curricular centralization

A family wants to know whether a child is developing the capacity to read fluently and understand complex material. The desired outcome is substantive literacy, not a new federal program.

A local educator and school may need high-quality instructional resources, valid assessment and support for individual learning needs. State authorities may set relevant requirements and establish accountable public systems. Federal statistical and research functions can publish reliable comparative evidence and disseminate rigorous findings, while Congress can review the legality and effectiveness of any applicable federal support.

No institution in this chain is permitted to infer that national literacy aims authorize federal officials to choose a local classroom's specific instructional materials. The remaining research questions concern which evidence best captures applied reading ability and which locally chosen approaches produce durable outcomes.

### 5.2 Vocational advancement as a connected educational pathway

A learner wants to develop technical skill, pursue work, and later return to a degree program or a different occupation. Schools, postsecondary institutions, employers and state workforce bodies may form parts of this pathway; DOL and recognized state apprenticeship agencies have specific registration and oversight roles where Registered Apprenticeship Programs are involved.

The national objective is a credible path to growth and portable capability. It does not depend on declaring apprenticeships or universities superior across all careers. Future research must study transfers, quality, cost, fair access, recognition of prior learning and the actual contributions of distinct government programs.

### 5.3 Disability rights and substantive educational opportunity

A child eligible under IDEA may be entitled to a free appropriate public education and related procedures under specific statutory provisions. Local and state education authorities have implementation and general-supervision roles. Federal OSEP has monitoring responsibilities. Disputes may involve administrative processes and courts subject to jurisdiction and exhaustion rules.

The long-term public objective includes both **legal compliance** and **meaningful educational growth**. Those are related, but not interchangeable: a formal process without appropriate learning benefit can be inadequate, while an attractive proposed improvement cannot bypass existing legal rights. [EIA-SRC-04](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-04-special-education-under-idea)

The appropriate analysis must consider eligible students' individual circumstances rather than asserting a universal quantitative educational target.

## 6. What must remain protected

- **Federalism and lawful roles.** Schools are not presumed to be federal executive offices. Legislative committees do not administer local curricula.
- **Tribal and territorial distinctions.** Tribal nations are governments, not program beneficiaries or state subdivisions alone; territorial arrangements differ in law and practice.
- **Rights and remedies.** IDEA, anti-discrimination law and other applicable guarantees establish enforceable protections in defined circumstances; aspirational universal educational objectives must remain identified as proposed norms.
- **Educational pluralism.** Families, educators and communities should have lawful voice and choices without compromising applicable child safety and rights.
- **Data privacy and statistical independence.** Learning analytics, student information and comparative statistics require proportionate, secure and honest use.
- **Outcome integrity.** Increased funding, credential counts or new offices are not themselves evidence of successful learning.
- **Public participation.** Meaningful community feedback can improve decisions; it should receive reasoned consideration rather than become a performative exercise.
- **Institutional adaptability.** An organization's future contribution may change without requiring its current structure to be treated as permanent or obsolete.

## 7. How this supports the wider national architecture

This is the first small-scale test of using **two complementary manuscript views**.

The future-state domain is organized around the public outcome. This institutional crosswalk is organized around public offices, law, accountability and contribution to outcomes. Both refer to the same educational concepts (EDU-O01–O10, EDU-C01–C12, EDU-P01–P08) but avoid reproducing an education policy chapter inside every institution profile.

The distinction will carry forward to health, economic opportunity, cybersecurity, defense and other domains.

| Architectural perspective | Education example | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L0 — Foundational principles | Liberty, dignity, meaningful opportunity, limited lawful authority, evidence | Proposed FP relationships |
| L1 — Institutional responsibility | Congress, executive functions, courts, state/local, tribal and territorial authorities | Selected roles sourced; detailed program authority incomplete |
| L2 — Public outcomes | EDU-O01–O10 and EDU-P01–P08 | Candidate indicators and conditions; not operational KPI baselines |
| FEAF Performance/Business | Learning results and functional education support relationships | Conceptual links only; no authorized government FEAF model created |
| FEAF Data/Security | Confidential records, independent assessment, access boundaries | Research and modeling pending |
| Future implementation | Statute, financing, state/community action, pilot, verification | Intentionally **not prescribed** in INC-04 |

The operating principle is **one future-state chapter, many lawful institutional contributors, one traceable source record**.

## 8. Material gaps and next questions

The principal gaps are not reasons to invent a new federal education organization chart. They are the next research agenda:

1. Confirm the operational and legal authority for each 2026 interagency education agreement, including administration, funding control and remedies.
2. Prepare distinct maps for K–12, early childhood, higher education, workforce education and adult informal learning.
3. Compare state constitutional guarantees and school finance responsibilities without assuming one state's legal structure applies nationwide.
4. Document treaty/statutory and program distinctions for each tribe and BIE/tribally controlled school arrangement in consultation with affected institutions.
5. Produce individual territorial profiles and verify program eligibility, local governance and appeals.
6. Evaluate where existing independent educational evidence is sufficient and where methodology or sampling improvements are needed.
7. Design a way to publish citizen-experience evidence alongside official statistics without compromising privacy or overstating representativeness.
8. Trace specific civil-rights complaint and IDEA procedural remedies accurately without implying courts manage school policy.
9. Examine recognized options for linking technical, academic and lifelong learning without requiring a single credential system.
10. Identify which capabilities can be strengthened through lawful voluntary cooperation before considering additional central authority.

## 9. Editorial and public-review disposition

**Draft completeness:** The institutional crosswalk maps 10 education outcomes and 12 capabilities, introduces four branch/jurisdiction profiles and cites the authority register. It is a pilot of institutional presentation, not a comprehensive national agency inventory.

**Independent reviews requested:** Federal and state education law; congressional operations; special education and rights enforcement; tribal government and territorial administration; education practitioners; evidence and privacy professionals; public accessibility.

**Meaningful public feedback:** Readers should be able to question whether a mapped institution actually has the cited power, whether an outcome has been overlooked, whether a proposed relationship could harm autonomy or equity, and whether a different locally appropriate mechanism would work better. Subsequent revisions should record considered responses rather than quietly changing normative premises.

*This crosswalk expresses potential contributions by lawful institutions. It does not direct any branch of government, compel a school to change instruction, authorize public spending, or ratify an ARFI policy position.*
