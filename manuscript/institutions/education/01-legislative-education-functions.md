# Congress and Legislative Education Functions — Future-Role Profile

**The Republic We Build | ARFI**  
**Profile ID:** EDU-INST-LEG-01 | **Version:** 0.1.0 — conceptual draft  
**Level:** Legislative branch and congressional support institutions  
**Reference date:** October 16, 2026 | **Status:** Open to legal and public review

> **Public purpose.** Congress is where federal education responsibilities and financing may be lawfully debated and authorized, and where federal programs can be held accountable. It is not the operator of American classrooms. A stronger future depends on clear legislative authority, informed public deliberation, transparent spending and credible oversight, not a predetermined expansion of central control.

## 1. Institutional Identity and Present Lawful Role

Congress acts through the House and Senate under the Constitution and applicable legislative procedures. Education-related statutes, appropriations and oversight fall within multiple committee jurisdictions rather than one educational command.

The **House Committee on Education & the Workforce** describes jurisdiction over early learning, elementary and secondary education, special education, higher education, career education and workforce development. The **Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions (HELP)** describes jurisdiction over education, workforce and related programs. Committee jurisdiction organizes legislative work; it neither enacts law by itself nor grants management power over state schools. [Official committee sources EIA-SRC-05–06](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-05-house-legislative-education-jurisdiction)

Congressional appropriations decisions and federal program authorizations operate under constitutional and statutory limits. The **Government Accountability Office** independently investigates federal programs and advises Congress. A 2026 GAO report on K–12 oversight is an example of how independent evidence can inform review, not a final verdict about every education service. [EIA-SRC-07](../../../research/EDUCATION_INSTITUTIONAL_AUTHORITY_REGISTER.md#eia-src-07-congressional-independent-oversight)

The envisioned future is an accountable legislature capable of evaluating proposals from multiple communities, understanding the full cost and purpose of its commitments, and explaining decisions in terms people can examine.

## 3. Capabilities and Relationships

Congress may require well-supported analytical capacities for reviewing national learning evidence, comparing statutory programs, understanding state and tribal implications, assessing family and student impacts and asking whether funding conditions distort teaching or reporting.

Committees, appropriations processes, Member offices, and legislative support organizations contribute in different ways. Public hearings, written testimony, accessible remote participation and independent research are subjects worth examining for effective civic engagement. These are **possible practices or enhancements**, not statements that a particular hearing mechanism is currently mandated.

The constitutional link is deliberate: supporting national learning is not synonymous with running local education. Federal spending and oversight power are bounded by the Constitution, authorizing law and enacted appropriations; changes to these boundaries require separate legal examination.

## 4. Accountability, Rights, and Boundaries

- Preserve educational pluralism and rights guaranteed in applicable federal and state law.
- Treat statistics, testimony, family experience and dissent as different but relevant evidence.
- Avoid conflating expenditure, compliance reports and credentials with learning achieved.
- Evaluate the impact of financing conditions on local choice, disability services and geographic access.
- Make congressional reasoning and material fiscal implications understandable without disclosing protected private information.
- Do not present a House or Senate committee as a unilateral authority over state education programs or federal executive administration.

## 5. Concepts for Future Evolution

Possible conceptual directions include more accessible evidence-led deliberation, stronger comparison of educational outcomes rather than program activity, systematic analysis of statutory overlaps, and more transparent responses to important public comments.

Other possibilities include preserving federal approaches shown to work or reducing redundant reporting where it does not support useful accountability. No specific legislation, committee reorganization or spending change is adopted.

## 6. Implications for People and Institutions

Students and families should understand why a federal program exists, what it requires, whether it produces value, and how to report problems. States and localities should understand federal obligations and how they intersect with their own responsibilities. Tribal and territorial voices should be included based on their distinct legal status, not treated as a generic constituent group.

Congressional evaluation must also preserve the independent adjudicative role of courts and the distinct administrative responsibilities of executive organizations.

## 7. Questions and Evidence Needed

1. Which federal educational functions produce demonstrable value while respecting state and community authority?
2. Do Congressional performance measures capture meaningful learning rather than only compliance or spending?
3. Which program-specific funding conditions influence curricula, and are they legally supported?
4. Where do congressional committee jurisdictions or appropriations processes create avoidable duplication?
5. How should affected families, educators, adult learners, tribal communities and territories be heard without inviting automated manipulation or unevidenced generalizations?
6. Which current congressional procedures are effective and should be preserved?
