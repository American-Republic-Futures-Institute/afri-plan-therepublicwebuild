# Executive Summary — The Republic We Build

**American Republic Futures Institute (ARFI)**  
**Document ID:** ARFI-PLAN-FM-01 | **Version:** 0.1.0 | **Status:** Editorial draft; open for public and expert review  
**Planning horizon:** 2026–2101, with exploratory stress tests beyond 2101

> **The central proposition:** The United States should be judged by whether its institutions protect liberty, enable people to live securely and meaningfully, support broadly accessible prosperity, preserve democratic self-government, and sustain the capacity to defend those conditions. The purpose of reform is not to enlarge government for its own sake or dismantle it for ideological reasons. It is to make legitimate public authority more capable, constrained, accountable, and useful to the people.

## ES-01 — The problem is systemic, not merely administrative

Americans experience the republic through connected systems: the Constitution, elections and representation, courts and civil remedies, federal and state institutions, markets and employment, housing and healthcare, education, infrastructure, national defense, and public services. Each has its own law, history, actors, resources and incentives. Their outcomes, however, are interdependent. A change to taxation affects investment; investment affects employment and industrial capacity; industrial capacity affects infrastructure, national security, and the tax base. Changes to healthcare access can affect family stability, productivity, and local economic life. An attempt to improve security can simultaneously strengthen national resilience and threaten privacy if its authority is poorly designed.

It follows that a collection of disconnected policy proposals is not a sufficient strategy for a seventy-five-year transformation. Changes must be evaluated as interventions in a constitutional, economic, technical, and human system. They must have a defined legal route, responsible institutions, credible financing, citizen-centered outcomes, and mechanisms for detecting failure.

The American Republic Futures Institute (ARFI) proposes to develop an open, evolving architecture for that work. The initiative currently uses **United States 2100** as a working designation; its manuscript is titled **The Republic We Build**. Its first horizon runs from 2026 through 2101, but its structural principles are meant to be tested against more distant possibilities.

## ES-02 — The objective: a free, capable, prosperous, self-correcting republic

The desired republic is neither a minimal state indifferent to the conditions of life nor a centralized state granted unlimited authority in the name of public welfare. It is one in which the people retain substantive liberty, rights are enforceable, economic contribution is rewarded, essential services are dependable, and public institutions are competent and correctable.

A capable republic should be able to deter aggression, assist allies, defend critical infrastructure, and protect citizens from unlawful coercion and cyber threats. A free republic must also prevent security powers from becoming instruments of indiscriminate surveillance, political censorship, or unreviewable control. A prosperous republic should reward investment, enterprise, skill and work while ensuring that markets compete through quality, affordability and productive value, rather than through unlawful manipulation or captured institutions. A democratic republic should allow meaningful participation, fair representation, and peaceful opposition even when that opposition delays a preferred reform.

These objectives are complementary in some circumstances and in tension in others. The task of national architecture is to expose that tension and examine lawful, evidence-grounded choices rather than declare the tradeoffs solved in advance.

## ES-03 — Twenty proposed first principles

The initiative begins with twenty *proposed* first principles, organized here into five connected commitments. Their authoritative wording and identifiers appear in the companion introduction and the draft foundational charter.

**The individual and the community (FP-01–FP-04, FP-07–FP-09):** legitimate government derives from the people; human dignity and liberty constrain state power; equality before the law must be combined with meaningful opportunity; the law must provide remedy against coercive misuse; protections for persons must be distinguished from citizenship-specific political rights; essential conditions for human capability are public concerns; and community must be supported without compelled ideological conformity.

**Constitutional institutions (FP-05–FP-06, FP-18–FP-20):** federalism and separated powers prevent dangerous concentration; government effectiveness is measured in outcomes rather than administrative activity; evidence, dissent, and uncertainty must be recorded honestly; and institutional reform must preserve the republic's ability to correct its mistakes under changing conditions.

**Markets and advancement (FP-10–FP-12, FP-17):** productive competition, worker and consumer interests, entrepreneurship, discovery, environmental responsibility, and intergenerational fiscal credibility belong in the same framework. Neither private ownership nor public ownership automatically demonstrates good service; performance and lawful authority matter.

**Security and the world (FP-13–FP-16):** defense must remain formidable and constitutional; data security, privacy, and cognitive autonomy deserve attention; national sovereignty can coexist with constructive international partnership; and strategic resilience requires domestic capacity, distributed infrastructure, and trusted supply relationships where the evidence supports them.

**The unifying commitment:** every instrument of public power is subordinate to legal legitimacy, human dignity, verifiable public purpose, and continued democratic accountability.

## ES-04 — What this paper does not assume

This initiative does **not** presume that all present institutions are failures, that all market concentration is unlawful, that the federal government should operate every essential service, that surveillance is indispensable to defense, that all proposed social guarantees already exist as constitutional rights, or that national debt can be eliminated without real economic consequences.

It does not treat uncertainty about election integrity or government misconduct as proof of a specific allegation. Such claims require records, evidence, fair processes, and recognition of what remains unknown. It does not regard lawful political opposition as illegitimate obstruction. Nor does political-party independence imply absence of moral commitments: the inquiry is anchored explicitly in liberty, human dignity, equitable opportunity, constitutional government, and effective public service.

The choice of tax mechanisms, ownership models, statutory reforms, representation formulas, judicial-tenure proposals, campaign-finance changes, military capacity, intelligence safeguards, immigration rules and industrial subsidies remains open to legal, comparative, technical and empirical review.

## ES-05 — How the architecture is organized

The manuscript proceeds from basic constitutional commitments to institutional responsibilities, citizen outcomes, the capabilities through which government acts, and implementation. The proposed architecture is:

- **L0 — Foundational protections, constraints and first principles.** Which values, binding rights, proposed guarantees, and limits must shape all changes?
- **L1 — Institutions and lawful authority.** Who may act, what may they do, and what checks and remedies apply?
- **L2 — National outcomes.** What must improve for people, how is it measured, and who bears the costs and risks?
- **L3 — Enterprise architecture.** Which capabilities, activities, actors, information flows, applications, infrastructure, budgets, dependencies and safeguards are required? Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework (FEAF) reference models and DoDAF-inspired views are methodological inputs, not claims of government authorization.
- **L4 — Implementable change.** Which laws, amendments, budgetary instruments, institutional actions, transition plans, pilots, independent assessments and public-review controls are needed?

A proposal may require changes across several layers. Conversely, a desired outcome at L2 does not independently create legal power at L1.

## ES-06 — How reforms will be examined

ARFI proposes twelve documented review gates, G-00 through G-11: problem and provenance; lawful authority; rights and dignity; public value and causality; distribution and equity; security and abuse resistance; federalism and dependencies; fiscal and operational feasibility; alternatives and proportionality; public participation and expert challenge; transition protection; and verification and correction.

These gates distinguish threshold legal constraints from policy optimization. A favorable benefit estimate does not authorize the violation of a binding right. A reform without adequate evidence remains a hypothesis. A reform that requires constitutional amendment must say so. Alternatives include better enforcement of existing law, revised incentives, non-governmental solutions, direct public provision where lawful, or no immediate action. Every substantial recommendation must specify who benefits, who pays, who faces harm, and how the result can be evaluated and corrected.

The public should be able to inspect reasoning, submit objections, challenge assumptions and see the disposition of consequential comments. Public participation does not permit anonymous automated manipulation or unreviewed alteration of official manuscripts. The institute itself must disclose uncertainty, donor and reviewer conflicts, methodology limitations and dissenting interpretations.

## ES-07 — The coverage of the plan

The planned manuscript is organized into ten major parts: constitutional foundations; democratic institutions; government operations; human well-being and community; markets and public finance; industrial capacity and environment; defense and democratic security; foreign relations; science and future resilience; and implementation architecture. Appendices will maintain legal authorities, research sources, capability models, outcome definitions, fiscal assumptions, public decisions, and superseded alternatives.

This outline is a starting coverage map rather than a claim to have enumerated every government function. A national capability inventory will systematically test the manuscript's omissions and interfaces across legislative, executive, judicial, state, local, territorial and tribal institutions.

## ES-08 — Time horizon, milestones, and success

The first program checkpoints are **2026, 2031, 2041, 2051, 2076 and 2101**. They are review horizons, not guaranteed delivery dates or numerical forecasts. Initially the emphasis is on building credible legal, capability and evidence baselines. Later stages should test, implement, measure and revisit reforms that have demonstrated value. Beyond 2101, exploratory scenarios for 2200 and 2300 will test whether the founding principles withstand changes that cannot now be predicted with confidence.

Success cannot be reduced to aggregate GDP, agency budgets, legislative enactments, military expenditure, or the number of modeled capabilities. It must be established through changes in people's lives and the republic's ability to sustain those changes: security without repression, better access to dependable essentials, meaningful education, productive opportunity, accountable representation, competitive value, environmental and fiscal resilience, and reliable alliances. Measurements must report who benefits and who does not.

## ES-09 — Public invitation and institutional limits

This paper is a starting point for public inquiry, not a finished national consensus. Its arguments, architecture, sources and revisions should remain open to constructive challenge, including from those who disagree with its premises. The institute can publish research and recommendations; only duly authorized governmental bodies can implement laws or exercise public powers. Proposed ARFI governance, charitable status, editorial safeguards and public review processes themselves require lawful institutional adoption.

The standard for progress is therefore not how quickly the plan gathers adherents. It is whether the evidence improves, the constitutional and institutional architecture becomes clearer, competing interests are more honestly represented, and plausible reforms emerge that demonstrably benefit the public without sacrificing the protections that give government its legitimacy.

**The Republic We Build begins with this proposition: protect the people's rights, define the outcomes they should be able to achieve, constrain power, test reforms honestly, and preserve the nation's ability to correct itself.**
