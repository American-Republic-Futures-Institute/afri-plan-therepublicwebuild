# The Republic We Build — Master Manuscript Outline

**American Republic Futures Institute (ARFI)**  
**Document:** ARFI-PLAN-OUTLINE-001  
**Version:** 0.2.0 — proposed editorial refactor  
**Status:** Working outline; chapters, institutions and future-state concepts remain open to review  
**Horizon:** Long-term and intergenerational; outcome checkpoints are not fixed deadlines

> **Editorial purpose.** This paper begins with constitutional and human principles, describes the America people might seek to build across major national outcome domains, then examines the lawful roles of governmental branches and institutions. It closes with conceptual pathways, ongoing learning and public accountability. It is a **future-state inquiry**, not an agency audit or prescriptive implementation plan.

The outline is a living editorial index, **not the authoritative architecture database**. A chapter's place in the manuscript can change without altering its persistent identifier or underlying evidence. Detailed models belong in the separate architecture repository; supporting data and legal research may be cited from research records and appendices.

## Part II — The America We Seek to Build: National Future States

**Question:** What should improve in American life, what must become possible, and how would we recognize durable progress?

This is the manuscript's **principal substantive section**. Each domain begins with a clear desired national condition, then works backward to required capabilities, relevant evidence, alternatives and outcome-based conditions for progress. Current conditions are used to test assumptions and explain gaps; they are **not** the narrative center. Domain divisions are provisional and may change following public and expert review.

### DOM-01 — Democracy and Civic Life

**Future condition.** A republic in which every eligible participant can engage meaningfully in public decisions and every person can challenge governmental conduct through fair, understandable processes.

**Conditions for progress (not a required sequence):**

- Representation and civic participation are accessible and meaningful.
- Institutions provide reasoned responses to public concerns and preserve dissent.
- Legitimacy is sustained through trustworthy, auditable democratic procedures.

## Part III — The Government We Need: Branches, Institutions, and Offices

**Initial institutional exemplar:** [Education: how public institutions can contribute](manuscript/institutions/education/00-institutional-crosswalk.md), with four branch/jurisdiction profiles and a separately sourced authority register. This is a model of lawful roles and future contributions, not a proposed agency restructuring.

**Question:** Which public responsibilities and capabilities would support the national future states, and how should institutions remain constitutional, accountable and useful?

This is an **institutional reference view**, distinct from the future-state domains. It follows a recognizable governmental structure without presuming any department, agency or office must be perpetuated unchanged. An institutional profile should describe current lawful responsibilities accurately, then consider its potential future roles and required capabilities. It should *link to* domain chapters rather than duplicate their analysis.

### INST-A — Legislative Branch: Congress and Its Institutions

**Working office and function index:** House of Representatives; Senate; relevant committees and leadership functions; appropriations and legislative budgeting; congressional support and accountability offices such as GAO, CBO, CRS and the Library of Congress. The institutional role and legal status of each unit must be verified in its eventual profile.

**Future-role question:** What representative, legislative, resource, public-participation and oversight capabilities should Congress sustain or develop?

#### INS-02 — Congress as the representative and legislative institution

House apportionment and size, Senate design, committees, legislative capacity, constituent access, appropriations, and oversight.

#### ADM-04 — Public budgeting, appropriations, and financial controls

Budget formulation, apportionment, grants, outcome reporting, anti-fraud, and fiscal transparency.

### INST-B — Executive Branch: Presidency, Departments, and Agencies

**Working institutional hierarchy (an index for research, not proposed reorganization):**

- **President and Executive Office of the President:** constitutional executive leadership, coordinating offices, management and budget, national policy support, and lawful executive administration.
- **Cabinet-level departments for initial navigation:** State; Treasury; Defense; Justice; Interior; Agriculture; Commerce; Labor; Health and Human Services; Housing and Urban Development; Transportation; Energy; Education; Veterans Affairs; Homeland Security.
- **Independent and cross-government organizations:** separate profiles where warranted for bodies such as the Environmental Protection Agency, Social Security Administration, General Services Administration, Office of Personnel Management, Federal Communications Commission, Federal Reserve System, National Aeronautics and Space Administration, and U.S. Postal Service, with each body's *actual* authority, organization and independence reviewed individually.
- **Offices, bureaus and components:** when profiles are developed, use the actual institutional hierarchy (for example department → operating administration or bureau → office/function) and avoid assigning responsibilities based on department names alone.

**Future-role question:** What administrative, service, regulatory, coordination and operational capabilities are needed to support the national outcome domains? Any reallocation of authority remains a separate legal and public design question.

### INST-C — Judicial Branch: Courts and Judicial Institutions

**Working institutional hierarchy:** Supreme Court; U.S. courts of appeals; federal district courts; relevant specialized courts; Judicial Conference of the United States; Administrative Office of the U.S. Courts; Federal Judicial Center; and other judicial-support bodies as warranted. ### INST-D — The Federation and Other Constitutional Relationships

**Not a fourth federal branch.** Separate cross-jurisdictional profiles for states, local government, tribal nations and U.S. territories, preserving their distinct constitutional and statutory relationships. Treat non-federal entities as partners or independent authorities as applicable, not subordinate executive agencies.

#### INS-09 — Federalism and the states

#### INS-10 — Tribal nations, territories, and local government

### INST-E — Cross-Branch Checks and Institutional Interfaces

**Purpose:** Make institutional dependencies, oversight, accountability and authority conflicts visible without treating legitimate disagreement as a defect. Track which parts of a cross-government question are a constitutional duty, discretionary service, budget dependency or proposed redesign.

#### INS-01 — Constitutional map of the federal government

Enumerated, implied, delegated, inherent, shared, and prohibited authorities, with contrary interpretations.

#### INS-08 — Oversight and corruption prevention

#### INS-12 — Institutional deadlock and lawful correction

### Institutional profile standards

Every institution or office profile should provide: (1) current legal identity and jurisdiction; (2) high-level purpose and functions; (3) its contributions to the future-state domains; (4) capabilities and interfaces that may need to evolve; (5) rights, oversight and accountability boundaries; (6) dependencies and alternative structural concepts; (7) unresolved questions. The main manuscript should not reproduce detailed organization charts, regulatory inventories or program audits when a reference will suffice.

## How a Future-State Chapter Will Be Written

Every domain chapter will lead with the **desired public condition and human consequences**, followed by observable outcomes, needed capabilities, relevant evidence from today, gaps and dependencies, alternative ways forward, rights and distributional considerations, institutional links, and open questions.

- **Outcome checkpoint:** a capability or condition achieved and sustained, not a fixed date or mandated technical solution.
- **Current-state material:** enough verified context to inform the future-state case, with detailed assessments referenced rather than reproduced.
- **Government relationships:** linked to Part III and structured models, not duplicated across chapters.
- **Status and uncertainty:** proposed values, validated observations, policy alternatives and legal constraints remain distinguishable.
- **Publication constraint:** no chapter becomes law or ratified ARFI policy because it is written here.

See the companion coverage crosswalk for the unchanged chapter IDs and their v0.1.0 → v0.2.0 editorial relocation.
